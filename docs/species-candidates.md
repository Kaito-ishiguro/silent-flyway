# Species candidates

Which birds can actually carry this piece. Built by scanning every extracted
Hong Kong Bird Report for published "Peak counts by year" tables — **168
species have a clean annual series** — then filtering hard on two things a
league table cannot see: whether HKBWS itself calls the trend a decline, and
whether the bird makes a sound worth hearing.

Data need not be continuous. A species with a real figure every few years is
usable; the viewer dashes the interpolated stretches and the scoreboard shows
the last known count.

## Why a raw decline ranking is not enough

Ranking 168 species by first-value-to-last-value produced a top ten that was
substantially wrong, because a single exceptional year makes a stable species
look collapsing. HKBWS says so in its own commentary:

| Looked like a crash | What HKBWS actually says |
| --- | --- |
| Black Drongo, "90% down" | "peak counts are higher than in the 1990s with an exceptional count in 2010" |
| Red-billed Starling, "81% down" | "relatively stable since the 1990s except for exceptionally high numbers in the winter of 2006-07" |
| Chestnut Bulbul, "70% down" | "increased substantially in numbers since the 1990s" |
| Great Crested Grebe, "62% down" | "was increasing in numbers up to 2012" |
| Blue-tailed Bee-eater, "64% down" | "now recorded in higher numbers than in the 1990s" |
| Eurasian Skylark | "has increased substantially" |
| Greater Painted-snipe | "relatively stable since 1994" |

**Every species below has a decline stated by HKBWS, not merely inferred from
the numbers.**

## Tier 1 — build with these

| Species | Series | Trend | Voice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Common Shelduck** | 1999–2014 | 1,250 → **0** | Loud goose-like honks and whistles |
| **Eurasian Teal** | 1999–2014 | 5,411 → 619 | Famous bell-like whistle; abundant recordings |
| **Northern Pintail** | 1999–2014 | 8,086 → 1,410 | Soft whistles and quacks |
| **Eurasian Coot** | 1971–2014 | 3,245 (1992) → 48 | Explosive metallic *kowk*; unmistakable |
| **Oriental Reed Warbler** | 2003–2014 | 200 → 29 | **The sound of Mai Po reedbeds.** Loud, grating, incessant |
| **Yellow-breasted Bunting** | 1959–2014 | 3,000 → 120 | Already in the piece |

Shelduck is the centrepiece: HKBWS records a documented **0** in 2014, so the
extinction is in the data rather than inferred. Oriental Reed Warbler is the
strongest addition — it declined substantially since the 1990s and it is the
loudest thing in a Hong Kong reedbed, so its silence is genuinely felt.

## Tier 2 — strong additions

| Species | Series | Trend | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Eurasian Wigeon** | 1999–2014 | declining | Pure rising *wheeoo* whistle, very distinctive |
| **White-throated Kingfisher** | 2000–2014 | 46 → 23 | Loud laughing trill; **Xeno-canto has a Hong Kong recording** |
| **Black-capped Kingfisher** | 2000–2014 | 12 → 4 | Sharp cackling flight call |
| **House Swift** | 1990s–2014 | 2,000+ → 500 | Screaming flocks over the city — brings **urban** Hong Kong in |
| **Little Bunting** | 2003–2014 | 65 → 19 | Declined significantly since the 1990s; sibling to the yellow-breasted |
| **Zitting Cisticola** | 2000s–2014 | 50+ → few | Farmland; the *zit-zit-zit* song names the bird |

House Swift is worth taking seriously. It is the one bird here an urban
audience has actually heard, screaming over the rooftops, and HKBWS notes daily
counts of over 2,000 in the Avifauna period against 500 since 2006.

## Tier 3 — thematically vital, acoustically weak

| Species | Note |
| --- | --- |
| **Dalmatian Pelican** | Extirpated 2010. Has a 1999–2013 table after all. Silent in Hong Kong |
| **Black-headed Ibis** | Extirpated 1999. Only three counts. Silent away from colonies |
| **Black-faced Spoonbill** | Near-annual 1994–2026, the flagship, near-silent in winter |
| **Spoon-billed Sandpiper** | Has a 2000–2014 table under its **old name, *Eurynorhynchus pygmeus*** — the reason an earlier search missed it. 13 → 1. Thin voice |

These four tell the story but cannot sing it. If included, their recordings
would come from breeding grounds — true of the species, but not a sound this
bay ever made. That is a defensible artistic choice provided it is stated.

## Suggested build

**Twelve species — Tier 1 plus Tier 2** — gives:

- a **scale range** from 8,086 Pintail down to 4 kingfishers, so the lanes
  differ enormously in density
- **four habitats**: open water (ducks, coot), reedbed (warbler), farmland
  (buntings, cisticola), urban (swift) — which makes the colour coding mean
  something beyond decoration
- **one documented extinction inside the data** (Shelduck, 2014 = 0)
- every voice a real, recordable, distinctive sound

Add the Tier 3 four only if you want the extirpation dates on the rim, and
label the borrowed recordings honestly.

## Open leads

1. **2015–2018 reports** are listed on the HKBWS site but serve HTML rather
   than PDF; they need a browser session. That would extend every series by
   four years.
2. **Pre-1999** needs OCR — 1991 and earlier are scanned images.
3. The **1990–2014 annual bar charts** on pages 244–279 of the 2014 report
   cover roughly 220 species. Values can be read off them to about ±10%, which
   is `estimated`, not `documented` — but it would reach back to 1990.
